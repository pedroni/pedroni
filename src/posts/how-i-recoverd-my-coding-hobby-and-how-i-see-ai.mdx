I have to face reality: AI is here and it's changing paradigms. Coding is my hobby and [last year it seemed like I had lost my hobby and I had to find it again](/blog/learning-a-new-programming-language#trying-something-different). There is now a great frustation of those who enjoy coding, a skill that is a part of our identity was lost over night. AI can just do it.

## Now the AI can just do it

I'm constantly having to make an effort to not fall for that thought, because it's a trap. I have to keep it in my head that: AI is a tool, not a replacement. Use AI to do the boring, the repetitive tasks, be dilligent in what you like and what you don't like.

Work always went by like a breeze, I had fun at my job. However, it changed now. My hobby is no longer my work. I have to see them with different eyes.

## Learning in the era of AI

For new comers into software engineering. Learning will feel odd, there's so much noise nowadays. Doing something by hand sometimes will make you feel stupid, like wasting time on something meaningless.

I'd recommend everyone who is currently learning how to code to take a step back, and NOT use AI, do not use to generate code for you. Make an effort to solve problems by yourself. Use it ONLY after you've built things by yourself - alone - without a tutorial.

It's like when you're a kid, there is a reason why you were taught without a calculator. You have to understand the concepts, you have to understand the basics, before using the tool that will give out the answers, you have to make the connections in your brain to understand whats going on, so that you can be the one whos driving. The one in charge. You need to understand the logic behind what you're doing.

- if im saturated (like cruds) and already know how to do it, ill just use AI

## On recovering my hobby

I'm learning again, I'm after new concepts

I'm currently using AI as a learning partner, a mentor (kinda lonely though, but who would be 100% present and answer stupid questions? how would I even find someone that would do that willingly?). For me that's the best way I've used AI so far. Not for doing things for me, but to help me understand and to get the point where I can with confidence say: I built it. If AI would've written the code for me, it wouldn't be as fun. It wouldn't be as enganging. I would've keep on the vicious cycle of Vibe Coding: Making boring, frustated prompts, cursing an AI, for it to happily reply "You're right!" while doing the same wrong thing again. After all that, the AI finishes it. And I don't feel like it belongs to me.

Anyways... for C++ I'm writing things by hand, using books, documentation, and sometimes video tutorials. I'm learning again, it's a slow path, but one that I'm enjoying. At work, that's a different story, I care more about productivity, and that's probably why [last year it seemed like I had lost my hobby and I had to find it again](/blog/learning-a-new-programming-language#trying-something-different).
